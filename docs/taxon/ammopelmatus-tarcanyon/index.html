Figs 1178–1187, Tables 1 , 12 , 13 , 38 Species #26, Weissman 2001b . Distribution. (Fig. 1178). Only known from the base of the Kreyenhagen Hills, along Big Tar Canyon Rd in the Diablo Coast Ranges southwest of Avenal, Kings Co., and from Zapato Chino Creek area, 15 km NW of Tar Canyon, Fresno Co. , also in the Diablo Range foothills on the western side of the San Joaquin Valley. Recognition characters. Member of Fast Drumming Group. Medium-large to very large, megacephalic JC with prominent black abdominal bands, 2n ♂ =23. Distinguished from morphologically similar, nearby Fast Drumming Group A. bakersfield ( 116 km southeast), A. chimineas ( 100 km south), and A. grapevine ( 160 km southeast) by habitat and isolation. Distinguished from nearest DNA relative, A. danieli , by DNA , a separation of ~ 100 km and on different sides of the Coastal Ranges, and the finding of a sex clarification drum in A. tarcanyon . The largest specimen of any known Ammopelmatus species (See Fig. 3.2 in Weissman 2001a ) is probably this taxon. That poorly preserved, apparently teneral adult female was collected by Lloyd Bookout of the Kings Co. Department of Agriculture, with the following label data: California, Kings Co. (not Kern Co. as cited by Weissman 2001a ), Milhan [sic] Rd, 6 m S Avenal, date unknown but probably in 1980s (L. Bookout, pers. comm to DBW). This indicated area would be Milan Rd and probably near the Kettleman Hills sandy off-road areas visible from I-5. But on Google Earth, Milan Rd does not extend north near Avenal, being farther south closer to Buttonwillow. She measures, in mm, as follows: Body length 64.66, hind femur length 15.83, hind femur width 6.56, head height 20.21. Fore leg tibia with 2 ventral spurs; middle leg tibia with 8 calcars, 2 ventral spurs; hind leg tibia with 5 inner and 3 outer spines, 2 ventral spurs. Rear leg tibial calcars #3, 4, and 5 are distally expanded and spatulate. The nearest San Joaquin Valley population of another Fast Drumming Group taxon, is A. chimineas , located on the Chimineas Ranch, some 95 km due south of the Tar Canyon type locality. A. bakersfield type locality is some 100 km SE of Tar Canyon. At a grazed, rolling hill grassland site (S01-14, 35.951746° -120.165070°, 349m , 2.3 km NE of the type locality of A. tarcanyon ), on 26-ii-2001 , we collected 2 adult female A. nigrocapitatus while on that same day, we found no JCs at the type locality of A. tarcanyon . According to Wikipedia , The Kreyenhagen Hills are a range of foothills of the Diablo Range in western Fresno County and Kings County. The Kreyenhagen Hills form a long foothill belt in the soft formations between Reef Ridge and Kettleman Plain. They are divided into several groups, each a few miles wide, by streams crossing them at right angles. Kreyenhagen was the name of the family who originally owned the land in the area. According to ChatGPT, the name “Tar Canyon” likely derives from the presence of natural tar (or oil) seepage in the area, historically observed by Native Americans and early settlers.
