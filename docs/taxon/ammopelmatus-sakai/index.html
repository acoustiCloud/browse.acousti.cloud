Recognition characters. A small to medium-large, light colored, slow drumming (0.5 drums/s) JC only known from the coastal dunes at Ballona and the adjacent LAX El Segundo Dunes. 2n ♂ =23. Rear leg tibial calcar #2 is generally longer than calcar #1. These rear leg calcars can be a little spatulate. Easily separated from darker, sympatric A. ballonadark by color, drum rate, rear leg calcars, and DNA . Holotype . Male (in alcohol, Fig.677 ). Discussion. For every individual of A. sakai collected at the type locality, probably 2 or 3 individuals of A. ballonadark were seen. Both taxa were also collected in pitfall traps at nearby Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). Those 2 localities, separated by some 3 km , currently appear protected, but we chose Ballona as the type locality because we have more individuals, with karyotype and DNA information, than from LAX. On the other hand, the total area of these two sites is small, even for a JC species. And the easiest way to collect individuals is by turning over man-made debris, such as slabs of asphalt and cement, such that “cleaning up” these localities could make the study of A. sakai difficult. The technique perfected by Prentice et al. (2011) might be a good, alternative methodology to employ. The LAX dunes are part of the once larger El Segundo Dunes, and are one of only 3 remnants for the El Segundo blue butterfly (https://xerces.org/endangered-species/species-profiles/at-riskbutterflies-moths/el-segundo-blue), one of the first insects listed under the 1973 Endangered Species Act.
